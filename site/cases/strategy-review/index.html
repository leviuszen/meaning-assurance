First-party dogfooding case

Strategy is not exempt from evidence.

This case records a real internal discussion about how to communicate the project: candidate directions, adversarial questions, rejected claims, moderator decisions, and a final read-only audit.

This is first-party workflow evidence. It is not independent validation, market validation, or proof that the selected communication strategy will produce adoption.

The brainstorming frame

The discussion did not begin with one predetermined answer. It compared problem-first messaging, protocol-first positioning, product naming, evidence-led proof, and the risk of overstating “audit” or model consensus.

Adversarial questions preserved

  • Does the new name explain the capability, or only sound memorable?
  • Is agreement between two agents evidence—or synchronized confidence?
  • Does a first-party case prove the protocol works outside its authors?
  • Which claims map to implementation, and which remain positioning?

What the moderator decided

Keep the problem-first hook, restore technical restraint immediately after it, distinguish orchestration from governance, label the case as internal dogfooding, and keep final acceptance human.
